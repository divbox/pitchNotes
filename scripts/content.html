Arsenal drew 2-2 at Bayern on Wednesday. Eberechi Eze scored first and Alphonso Davies equalised before half-time. Just before the hour Jurriën Timber blocked a Harry Kane shot with his arm, and Kane scored the penalty. Declan Rice got the equaliser with about ten minutes left. Saturday was more comfortable. Arteta rested several players against Everton, and Arsenal still won 2-0 with two goals from centre-backs. Ezri Konsa scored his first for the club from a set piece, and Gabriel added the second after the break. City lost 2-1 at Aston Villa earlier that day, so Arsenal and City are now level on 21 points. City stay top on goal difference, 11 to Arsenal's 8.

United's week was about as bad as it gets. On Wednesday they lost 4-1 at Como. Nico Paz scored twice, and Joshua Zirkzee's goal came in stoppage time. Bruno Fernandes then said in public that Carrick's pressing was letting teams take over games, and Carrick left him on the bench for Bournemouth on Sunday. Carrick said the decision was about football and nothing else. Bournemouth won 3-0 at Old Trafford. Rayan and Marcus Tavernier scored before half-time and Evanilson added the third. The crowd booed at half-time, and at full time the Stretford End turned on the Glazers, Sir Jim Ratcliffe and Jason Wilcox. United are 16th on 7 points, one point clear of the bottom three. Their last win was 4-0 against Sabah in the Champions League on 10 September, seven games ago.

The clocks went back on Sunday, so UK time and UTC are the same again until March. United are at Chelsea at 12:30pm on Saturday 31 October. City host Brighton at 3pm the same day. Arsenal go to Liverpool, who are fourth, at 4:30pm on Sunday 1 November. In the Champions League, United host Roma at 8pm on Tuesday 3 November, and Arsenal are at Slavia Prague at 8pm the next night. The weekend after that, Arsenal host Hull at 3pm on Saturday 7 November, and United are at home to Aston Villa at 4:30pm on Sunday 8 November.
Bukayo Saka hurt his thigh in Munich and came off within about ten minutes. Arteta said "I think it will be weeks", and he'll be rescanned in the next week or so. He missed Everton and won't play at Anfield. Arteta said William Saliba is back training on grass after his back injury and called the signs "very, very positive". He's due another scan this week, and reports still expect him back in December rather than this weekend. Gabriel was fouled hard by James Tarkowski against Everton, and Arteta said he seems fine. Arteta also got his second yellow card of the season for leaving his technical area. Afterwards he said he keeps footage of other Premier League managers doing the same thing.
Carrick is still in charge. After the Bournemouth game most outlets expected him to be sacked. Omar Berrada and Wilcox held talks at Carrington on Monday, and the BBC and The Athletic both report that the club decided to keep him for now. Fabrizio Romano says United won't take "any official action", but they've started sounding out other managers. He names Eddie Howe, Unai Emery and Antonio Conte as the main candidates. One Italian report quotes Romano saying Conte has agreed a deal. Last week Romano said Conte had no agreement with anyone, and no English outlet has confirmed it, so treat it with caution. Yahoo Sports calls Saturday's trip to Chelsea crucial for him. Gary Neville's verdict after Sunday was blunt: "The club is a mess."
A Daily Briefing report says the Leipzig left-back is now United's first choice for January, ahead of Alejandro Balde, Adrien Truffert and Jarrad Branthwaite. Raum's contract runs out next summer and he's said to be keen. Romano has said United will spend in January only if results stay poor and the right players are available. The first condition is now met. Nobody else has backed up the Raum report yet, so it stays speculative.

When the Stretford End got angry on Sunday, they didn't aim it at the players. They sang at the Glazers, at Sir Jim Ratcliffe and at Jason Wilcox. That's because United have two sets of owners, and it isn't obvious who decides what.

The Glazer family has owned United since 2005 and still holds the majority of the shares. In 2024 Ratcliffe's company INEOS bought a minority stake of about a quarter of the club. Part of the deal was that INEOS would run the football side. So the people who hire and fire the head coach are Ratcliffe's appointees: Omar Berrada, the chief executive, and Wilcox, the director of football. Those were the two in Monday's meeting.

Fans tend to blame the Glazers for the debt and the state of Old Trafford, and Ratcliffe's team for the football. If Saturday goes badly at Chelsea, the decision about Carrick belongs to Berrada and Wilcox. The fans will hold them responsible for whatever comes next too.
