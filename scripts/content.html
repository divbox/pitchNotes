United were behind at Atlético Madrid before the first minute was out. Pablo Barrios scored it. Johnny Cardoso had a second ruled out by VAR for a handball in the build-up, so it stayed 1-0 until Leny Yoro was sent off as the last man for bringing down Jonathan David. Ademola Lookman scored straight after and it finished 2-0. United have gone five games without a win in all competitions.

The result was only part of the story. Lisandro Martinez said afterwards that the performance was poor and that it said something about how the club is run. He named the technical director, Jason Wilcox, and said he expected more investment. When asked about Michael Carrick, Martinez said the manager was doing his best. Two days later Martinez said on Instagram that he had met the club's leadership and the manager, and reports say he apologised. David Ornstein of The Athletic says the owners are still behind Carrick. He also pointed out that every club backs its manager until the day it doesn't.

Arsenal had a much easier week. Lille kept them out for an hour at the Emirates, and then Arsenal scored three times in seven minutes. Jurriën Timber got the first from a Noni Madueke cross, Eberechi Eze added the second, and Bukayo Saka got the third after coming off the bench. Lille didn't have a shot on target. In the league, Arsenal are second on 15 points, three behind Manchester City. Coventry beat Newcastle 4-1 on Monday night. Arsenal also didn't sign the left-winger they wanted in the summer, and some reporters think that's a bigger need.

When Martinez criticised the club, he didn't aim at the manager. He named the technical director. Many Premier League clubs now split the football side into two jobs. The head coach picks the team, runs training and handles match days. A sporting or technical director sits above or beside the coach and looks after the longer-term work: recruitment, scouting, the academy pathway and the overall style the club wants to play.

That split is why a player can criticise the club and back the manager in the same breath. If the squad is short at left-back or up front, that's a recruitment question, and recruitment belongs to the director's department. The coach works with the players he's given.

The idea is that the director stays in post when a coach is sacked, so the club's plan doesn't restart every time it changes manager. The risk is that the director often chose the coach too, so a bad run can end up as a judgement on both of them. That's where United are now.
